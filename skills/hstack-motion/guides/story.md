# The story

A product video is short (30–60 s for a teaser, 1–2 min for an explainer) and has one job: make the
viewer want to try the product. It works when every scene has one idea and the order builds.

## Structure that works
1. **Introduce:** "Introducing <Product>." Said plainly, with the logo. Not over-excited.
2. **The problem, shown:** the real way people suffer today, as a scene they recognise. For a secrets
   manager: teams pasting `.env` files in Slack, WhatsApp and Discord. Start straight away ("Right
   now…"), no "Because…".
3. **Make it sting:** what goes wrong, quickly. ("Copied, pasted and forgotten. No way to track which
   is which. Just confusion and security risks.")
4. **The fix:** "<Product> fixes that." Then the one mechanism that matters (encryption, sync…),
   shown as motion, not described.
5. **What you do with it:** the core verbs as one rolling phrase ("Push, pull, share and run."),
   each with the real command or screen.
6. **Next to the alternatives (optional):** name them fairly ("Both are great tools, built to do a
   lot."), then what this product chooses to be ("If all your team needs is X, <Product> does just
   that.").
7. **Price (optional):** like with like (hosted against hosted), as bars, with the product's share of
   each price and a dated footnote.
8. **The other way to use it (optional):** cloud first, then self-hosted as the private option.
9. **Who it's for**, then **the logo and the website**. End on the URL, not an install command.

Leave out any step the product has no honest answer for.

## Script rules
- Short sentences, written to be heard. Read every line aloud.
- Lists flow as one phrase: "push, pull, share and run", not "Push them. Pull them." (separate
  sentences make the voice pause and pitch up).
- No commas where the user doesn't want a pause ("for developers and engineering teams").
- The brand name is said plainly.
- Never downplay a competitor; never claim something unique that a competitor also has (open source,
  self-hosting, a free tier). Check their site.
- Every number on screen comes from the code or a dated source.

## Pacing
- 2–5 seconds a scene; the voice decides the length (`guides/voice.md`).
- Nothing on screen waits for long after the voice ends (0.3 s tail).
- Vary the rhythm: a fast run of slams, then one held reveal.
